Research Track A - Experience and Storytelling
1. Study 2-3 interactive portfolios or point-and-click interfaces. Record how quickly visitors can find actual work.
    - Nomadic Tribe website: Easy to "enter" and easy to see the mouse (they use a small circle that gets bigger when hovering over a clickable object)
    The sound is very cool and there is speech right away with words to read as the speak. As you move the mouse the screen reacts, it also gives queues as
    to what you can do in each scene. For the second scene you can "drag" to sprout flowers from the ground. The scene is also actively moving which makes
    it feel more real, the sound can also be turned off and it relatively easy to find. 
    Scene: Still, but objects moving such as plants, you advance vertically "into the screen"
    Cons: You can't skip things, so if you leave the site you have to go back to the start and go through the story again.

    - Noel.heschung.com website: Translation from French to English is available which is very cool. Sound is easy to find. Controls are labeled
    and have symbols to help. Hamburger menu available that allows you to traverse to different chapters. No skipping within the scene. You only have to press
    to move the person once, you can't stop the person once they start,  for the movements I clicked the space bar even though it should have been right arrow
    and it still moved.
    Scene: Moving horizontally across screen, minimalistic, you use controls on the keyboard to move the character. Strictly black and white.

    Sources used by developers: Custom code, React, WebGL, three.js, Webflow, Lottie, GSAP, Vue, Howler.js to name a few.

Research Track B - Visual Language (google search, Figma, and stockcake for images)
1. Collect 8-12 images showing original cozy, cinematic interiors: lighting, materials, perspective, and characters.
    The images I chose are reflected in my moodboard on 11/18/2026. I chose these images because they mimic a reality that is fun, cozy, and academic.
    All of these are things that I would use to describe myself and I am excited to see it come together.
2. Build a moodboard with three categories: architecture/furniture, lighting/color, and character/dialogue.
    The images I chose are reflected in my moodboard on 11/18/2026. I chose these images specifically because they reflect the atmosphere and reality
    I want to build in my website. The lighting is soft/yellow-amber, the architecture is vintage/antique but still modern, the characters are studious and
    realistic but still have a creative style to them i.e., animated but realistic.
3. Define 5 - 7 core colors and two type roles: expressive title style and readable interface style.
    The colors I chose are reflected in my color board on 11/18/2026. I specifically pulled these colors from the images I chose in my moodboard because they
    help build the atmosphere (cozy, cinematic, fun, and academic).
4. Decide whether the room uses a straight-on view, angled perspective, or isometric view. Choose One.
    I chose to use an eye-level cinematic perspective, I really want the user who I will refer to the "visitor" to experience the room from a viewpoint closer
    to where a person would stand or sit, as opposed to an isometric viewpoint.
